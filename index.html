About Me

I'm a PhD candidate in cognitive neuroscience at Temple University, working with David V. Smith in the Neuroeconomics Lab. I study how aging changes the way people process rewards and make decisions—and why that makes older adults vulnerable to financial exploitation, a problem responsible for over $40 billion in annual losses in the U.S.

My dissertation, funded by an NIH F31 fellowship from the National Institute on Aging, tests whether noninvasive brain stimulation (tACS) targeting prefrontal cortex can improve reward-based learning and reduce confirmation bias in older adults. This work aims to both clarify the neural mechanisms of exploitation vulnerability and develop a potential intervention.

More broadly, I investigate how individual differences in reward sensitivity shape behavior and brain function across the lifespan. I've contributed to three publicly released neuroimaging datasets on OpenNeuro (500+ participants), and my work draws on fMRI, computational modeling, and machine learning methods.
