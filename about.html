About

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Psychology at Temple University, where I work with Dr. David V. Smith in the Neuroeconomics Lab, supported by an NIH F31 fellowship from the National Institute on Aging. My work uses fMRI, transcranial alternating current stimulation, and computational modeling to ask how people learn from rewards, how they adapt when the rules change, and why that flexibility declines with age. I am especially interested in corticostriatal reward processing, reversal learning, and decision-making across the lifespan.

I completed my undergraduate work in Psychology and Neuroscience at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I worked with Dr. Charlotte A. Boettiger, and a master's in Experimental Psychology at Appalachian State University, where I worked with Dr. Andrew Monroe.

An overview of what I'm working on is in the pages above, with a fuller record on my CV. I can be reached at james[dot]wyngaarden[at]temple[dot]edu.
