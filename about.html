About Me

I am an incoming postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Psychiatry at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, where I work with Dr. Muhammad A. Parvaz in the MAP (Motivated Attention and Perception) Lab. My research focuses on understanding how reward learning deficits contribute to substance use disorders, using EEG, computational modeling, and transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS). I am particularly interested in why drug cue reactivity intensifies during abstinence and whether personalized neuromodulation can disrupt it.

I completed my PhD in Psychology at Temple University, where I work with Dr. David V. Smith in the Neuroeconomics Lab, supported by an NIH F31 fellowship from the National Institute on Aging. My doctoral work used fMRI, tACS, and computational modeling to study reward processing and decision-making across the lifespan.

I completed my undergraduate work in Psychology and Neuroscience at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I worked with Dr. Charlotte A. Boettiger, and a master's in Experimental Psychology at Appalachian State University, where I worked with Dr. Andrew Monroe.

An overview of what I'm working on is in the pages above, with a fuller record on my CV. I can be reached at james[dot]wyngaarden[at]temple[dot]edu.
